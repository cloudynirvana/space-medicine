On the space station, an astronaut takes an antibiotic. Does it work the same way up there?
Two organs decide its fate. The liver breaks drugs down. The kidneys flush them out.
So I pooled live NASA data. Seven missions. Over twenty thousand genes.
In space, the liver enzyme that clears erythromycin and clindamycin rose about thirty percent. The one that clears caffeine fell by almost a third.
The kidney's drug pumps held steady.
These are mouse genes, not people. But it's a hypothesis worth testing before Mars.
